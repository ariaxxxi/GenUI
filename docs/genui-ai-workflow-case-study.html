Designing with a prototype that could change itself

01 · Context

The prototype was not only a deliverable. It became the design environment.

The original challenge was to explore generative UI on a constrained glass canvas. Static mockups were too slow for the number of questions the work raised: What happens when a card becomes a pill? How should a bubble collapse? Where should an action row live? How much shimmer is too much?

Instead of treating code as a handoff step, I used AI to build a tool around the design problem. The tool let me edit stages, components, shell visibility, action rows, background media, lens states, and motion behavior directly in the browser.

The shift was subtle but important: I was no longer asking AI to make a final screen. I was asking AI to help me create a space where I could repeatedly judge, tune, and reframe the screen.

The design process moved from "make me an option" to "make me an instrument I can play."

03 · Workflow

The loop became faster, but also more critical.

AI changed the speed of implementation, but the design work did not disappear. The harder task became judging the output precisely: naming what felt wrong, turning that feeling into a constraint, then asking for a narrower change.

Example of the kind of motion detail that became editable: a fired bubble scales up, collapses toward center, fades faster than the geometry, and disappears in sequence.

01

Feel the problem

I watched the prototype in motion and described the mismatch: too jumpy, too fast, overlapping, not centered, wrong visual weight.

02

Make it concrete

The critique became a measurable instruction: 3s shimmer, 50px blur, 64px buttons, bottom aligned action row, 2s toast.

03

Patch the tool

AI helped implement the change directly in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript while preserving the existing system behavior.

04

Judge again

The next pass was not a review of a static comp. It was a live evaluation of timing, hierarchy, and interaction feel.

04 · Takeaways

AI did not replace the designer. It changed where design judgment happens.

The most useful AI collaboration was not a single impressive generation. It was the accumulation of small, specific adjustments that let the prototype become more sensitive to the design intent.

In this workflow, the designer's role shifts toward framing, taste, constraints, and critique. The AI accelerates execution, but the designer still decides what is worth making, what feels wrong, and when a behavior is finally coherent.

Prototype the process

When the design space is uncertain, build controls before polishing screens. The right tool can reveal the right design.

Use AI for iteration density

The advantage is not that AI makes one perfect answer. It makes it practical to evaluate many small behavioral changes.

Keep taste in the loop

Vibe coding works when the designer can name what the vibe is, what violates it, and what constraint should guide the next patch.
